# Landscape editorial guide

## Criteria

Compare light, atmosphere, depth, tonal relationships, structure, composition, and artistic intent. Prefer a varied top rather than many near-identical treatments of the same view.

## Counterexamples and artistic exceptions

Fog, silhouettes, low contrast, long-exposure water, minimalism, and intentional camera movement can be successful. Literal sharpness or high contrast is not a universal requirement.

## Escalation

Escalate uncertain intent, irrecoverable significant-region exposure, and ties whose differences depend on full-resolution detail. An artistic hypothesis remains a hypothesis until the result is evaluated.

## Coverage

Use the requested locations, viewpoints, light conditions, or story beats as coverage labels. Do not infer a missing place or time from visual similarity.

## Comparison with acceptable alternatives

A wide atmospheric view and a tighter structural study may both be acceptable. Similar compositions under meaningfully different light can remain useful alternatives when the brief values change over time.
